Executive Summary

DIPTI is an established professional training institute (founded in 2004) with a marketing website (dipti.com.bd) and a separate academic portal (academic.dipti.com.bd). Neither platform currently offers any AI or chat support.

We have already built and tested a working AI agent — Muse Glimmer (Meta's 30B open agentic model) — capable of chat, tool-calling, and image/video generation. This proposal shows how to roll it out in phases to grow enrollment, improve student performance and retention, support students through job placement, and open new revenue streams — while keeping risk low at every step.

Problem & Opportunity

  • No chatbot on the website — answering prospective students' questions requires a human, so nights and holidays go unanswered
  • Giving every student personalized guidance is time-consuming for trainers — the same questions come up again and again
  • There's no system to track whether a student is actually studying, or where exactly they're getting stuck
  • After finishing a course, job-hunting is entirely on the student — there's no connected career support
  • This is a clear gap compared to competitors like GoEdu and BSDI — and a clear opportunity

The Proposed Solution — A 5-Phase Rollout

01
dipti.com.bd · low cost, fast to demo

Enrollment AI Chatbot

A 24/7 chatbot on the marketing site — answers prospective students' questions, recommends the right course, and captures leads.

Why first: it shows a directly measurable impact through improved conversion rate — the most effective first step for convincing decision-makers.

02
academic.dipti.com.bd · the core of this proposal

AI Tutor & Personalized Learning Companion

  • Performance Tracking: monitors project submissions, task completion, quiz scores, and GitHub repo activity — real engagement data, not just self-reported progress
  • Multi-Stakeholder Dashboard: trainers see class-wide progress, admins see institute-wide KPIs, and parents see their child's specific progress — building trust and improving retention
  • Student Self-Assessment: students can test their own understanding anytime and get instant feedback
  • Personalized Roadmap + AI Guidance: the agent actively helps each student work through a structured roadmap, and suggests solutions whenever they get stuck
  • Activity-Based Content Delivery: analyzes activity logs to detect struggle patterns and proactively suggests the right next content or practice task
  • Institutional Feedback Loop: by analyzing patterns across all students, the system suggests to the curriculum team which features or interventions would most improve outcomes
03
New layer · placement-linked revenue

Career Services & Job Placement

  • RAG-Based Job Placement: analyzes each student's skill profile and course-completion data to surface relevant job openings on a personal dashboard
  • Profile/CV Builder: AI helps students build a profile and CV tailored to a specific job
  • All on one unified platform — the message: "We don't just train you, we stay with you until you're hired."
04
internal · operational savings

Internal Content-Generation Tools for Staff

Turns capability we've already built (PDF/PPTX and quiz generation) into an internal tool for trainers and the content team — cutting the time and cost of building new courses.

05
ambitious · B2B

White-Label Licensing

Package the entire system and license it as a SaaS product to partner institutes such as GoEdu, BSDI, and HRDI — a new revenue line beyond training itself.

Why cost per student falls so fast: the server's fixed monthly cost barely moves as students are added — one RTX 4090 comfortably handles roughly 1,000-1,500 active students on a chat-dominant workload before a second GPU is needed. Compute itself is cheap: about $0.07-0.08 per student per month for a full month of AI tutoring, occasional diagrams, and optional video.

Rent vs. buy: at ~$330/month for GPU compute alone, one RTX 4090 (~$1,700 retail) pays for itself in about 5-6 months of steady use — worth revisiting once Phase 2 is past the pilot stage.

Ways We Already Cut Cost — and What's Next

  • Right-sized models (done): FLUX.1-schnell instead of the larger FLUX.1-dev, and LTX-Video (~6GB) instead of HunyuanVideo (~50GB) — same task, a fraction of the compute per request
  • Fast-response mode (done): disabling Muse Glimmer's reasoning trace for routine chat cuts response time from ~14s to ~2s — directly cutting GPU-seconds billed per message
  • Gate the expensive feature: keep chat and image generation open to all students; make video generation a premium-tier feature, since it consumes the most compute per use
  • Cache and reuse: generate shared course material (diagrams, quiz banks) once per course, not once per student
  • Batch the non-urgent work: run content refreshes and auto-grading as nightly batch jobs instead of always-on real-time generation
  • Scale to zero during the pilot: spin the GPU down outside class hours until usage is steady enough to justify 24/7 uptime

Data privacy: student performance data — especially what's shared with parents — is sensitive, so proper access controls and consent are essential regardless of scale.
